# Faces

The face belongs to the character, not to a style. All sixteen faces — the three plain ones, `cute`, `eyes` and `line`, and thirteen [drawn faces](#drawn-faces) — are drawn in the vector, pixel and kaomoji series alike, on the same states, poses and blinks. The default is `cute`.

```tsx
<CharacterMark shape="mochi" face="line" nose="hook" />
```

| `face`           | Drawn as                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `cute` (default) | White dot eyes, a small mouth and pink blush painted on the body; some states also throw off a mood effect. See [Expressions](/design/expressions). |
| `eyes`           | Two cut-out eyes and nothing else — the silhouette does the rest.                                                                                   |
| `line`           | A nose and a mouth on top of the eyes, all strokes at one line weight.                                                                              |

`cute` is the default; pass `face="eyes"` or `face="line"` for the other plain faces, or a drawn face's id for one of the [drawn faces](#drawn-faces). `eyes` and `line` have their own geometry and eye tables. Switching `face` on a mounted character does not jump: the `cute` face and the others cross over on the `cuteBlend` spring.

## Drawn faces

The thirteen faces in `DRAWN_FACE_STYLES` are of the cute family: they keep the cute face's dot radius, eye line, cheeks and mood effects, and keep the body whole in the belt states. What differs is that their eyes are drawn as several layers, each with a paint role — whites, pupils, lids, brows, lenses, screens, symbols — and that each is a complete face: its own eyes for every state, its own mouths (or the cute mouth table, shared by name), its own layout, blink and gaze, and its own pixel and kaomoji renditions.

### One design table

Each drawn face is one `FaceDesign` in `face/drawn/designs/<id>.ts`:

- `layout`: in cute dot radii, measured from the cute eye line — the lift, the eye spacing, the mouth's place, size and weight, the cheeks, and how much of the gaze moves the whole eye and how much is left for the pupils.
- `expressions`: each expression is a handful of numbers. Every face draws all nineteen cute expression names in its own way, so state tables can be shared by name; `screen` and `symbol` add expressions of their own (a spinner, a magnifier, …).
- `states`, `mouths`, `stateMouth`: the expression cycle and the mouth of each state. A face that shares the cute tables says so — `states: STATE_CUTE_EYES`, `mouths: CUTE_MOUTHS`; `screen` and `symbol` override the busy states on top of the cute cycle, and `bigmouth` gives a mouth to every state the cute face leaves bare.
- `mouthPaint`, `quietFx`, `kaomoji`: the mouth's paint role, the effects the eyes already show (`glossy` cries its own tears, so it quiets `tear`), and the face in text.
- `eye` (and optionally `plate` and `inMouth`): numbers to layers.

Expressions blend field by field, so on a state change a part grows or fades in rather than appearing; each face decides whether a blink closes its lids or squashes the eye.

### Paint roles

Layers carry a paint role, not a colour. `face` is the cute face's colour (the whites), `pupil` what reads on it, `feature` what reads on the body (brows, lashes, eyes drawn straight onto the body — the soft variant's face colour), `glint` the light that reads on `feature`, `iris` the body's ink deepened, and the rest fixed accents (a gold star, a tear, a tongue, a heart, an anger mark, a button's thread and face, dark glass) and a display's light. A black body turns with the page, so `getInkVars('black')` also writes `--aora-face-pupil` and `--aora-face-glint`, and static output switches them with its colour scheme.

<DrawnFacePalette />

### Three series

- Vector: each variant says what every role is painted with. On the filled variants the whites are the face colour; on the line variants — `outline`, `glass`, `pencil`, `neon`, `chalk` — every role is the line or the page, and whites, lenses and screens get a rim; a variant that throws its effects in one colour turns the accents to the line too. Riso's misregistration, the paper shadow and the sticker's edge apply to the body as always.
- Pixel: each layer is measured by coverage and drawn whole or not at all. Both eyes go through one mirrored projection at anchors snapped symmetric about the axis, so a symmetric expression is symmetric to the cell while a wink, a glance or a one-sided tear keep their difference; a highlight smaller than a cell is left out rather than punching a hole in the pupil.
- Kaomoji: each face has its own eye glyphs, one per kind of eye (eleven kinds); a state's eyes are the first expression of its cycle, its mouth the state's mouth.

<DrawnFaceSeries />

### Narrow bodies

A design is drawn at its own size. Before it goes onto a body, the outline of every fixed part — brows, frames, visor, symbols, and the mouth's width — is measured and tested where it lands, through the head's projection: every band has to lie inside the body's width at its height and below hair or a leaf capping the head. A face that does not fit walks one path that shrinks it (to 0.45 at most) and lowers it (by up to 1.6 dot radii) together, and bisection finds the point where it just fits; the mouth and the cheeks move with it. Parts that drift free of the face (a falling tear, a rising Z) are not counted. On a body with room, the face keeps its drawn size.

## cute: the default face

The `cute` face is painted on the body in `--aora-face` (white on every hue; see [Palette](/design/palette#variables-beyond-the-ink)). Every state has its own eyes, mouth and blush, and the eyes cycle on an interval. The tables and demos are on [Expressions](/design/expressions); the mood and activity effects of each state are on [States](/design/states).

On the `cute` face the belt states do not shrink the body to a bead: the whole character, face included, stays, and what the belt says on the other faces is said by an effect beside it.

## The line face

`line` adds a nose and a mouth to `eyes`. The sections below are about it: how it keeps one ink and one weight, what the mouth and nose are, and how they follow the state.

## One ink, one weight

The `eyes` face works because it is a single visual idea: a filled body and two cut-outs. Adding a nose and a mouth risks turning that into a drawing with several kinds of line on it. The line face avoids that by keeping the rule:

- Every feature is a stroke in the eye colour — the background on `solid`, the ink on `outline` and `glass`.
- Every stroke has the same weight, `LINE_WEIGHT` times the eye radius, so it scales with the body exactly as the eyes do.
- The eyes shrink towards dot proportions (`LINE_FACE_TUNE`) so three features share the plate instead of crowding it, and the thin expressions — `closed`, `happy`, `sleepy` — are floored to the line weight so they read as the same stroke as the mouth.
- There are no brows, no blush and no second colour.

<LineExpressionGrid />

Every eye topology of the `eyes` face is available on the line face; it only changes how they are sized and floored. `wink` is the one topology whose two eyes differ — a happy arc on one side, a neutral eye on the other.

## The mouth is six numbers

```ts
interface MouthSpec {
  width: number; // half-width, in eye radii
  bend: number; // bow of the lip line; positive smiles, negative frowns
  open: number; // height of the opening; 0 is a closed line
  wave: number; // one sine ripple along the lip, for unease
  skew: number; // pushes the apex towards one corner, −1..1
  dy: number; // vertical offset from the resting position
}
```

The lip line is a parabola through the two corners, bowed by `bend` with its apex pushed sideways by `skew`, plus one ripple of amplitude `wave`. The opening is a lens split unevenly — the lower lip drops further than the upper lip rises, the way a real mouth opens. When `open` is 0 the two lips coincide and only the stroke is visible, so a closed mouth opening into an "o" is one continuous shape rather than a swap.

<MouthGrid />

<MouthTable />

Like the eyes, the mouth is parametric for a reason: **morphing**. Every field interpolates independently and every intermediate value is a valid mouth, so a state change eases from `frown` to `grin` on a spring instead of cutting between two outlines.

```ts
morphMouth(MOUTHS.frown, MOUTHS.grin, t); // always a mouth
```

## The nose is chosen once

A nose is not an expression. It is a trait, like a shape, so it is chosen once per character and stays put while the eyes and mouth change.

<NoseGrid />

Six polylines. Each is a single open stroke down the bridge with a small turn for the tip, stored as sixteen points in eye-radius units. Passing `nose={null}` draws none — a plain line face with eyes and mouth only.

When no nose is given, one is derived from the character's key the same way its shape and ink are: `autoNoseForKey('agent-42')` is stable and spreads keys evenly across the six. `AgentPortrait` uses this, so every agent gets a consistent nose with nothing stored.

## Every state has a mouth

`STATE_MOUTH` names one mouth per state, in the same way `STATE_EYE_TOPOLOGIES` names the eyes. The eyes carry most of the expression already, so the mouth's job is to agree with them — a frown under `sad`, an "o" under `surprised`, a wavy line under `confused` — rather than say something on its own.

<LineStateGrid />

Two mouths exist for states the other eleven could not say honestly. `hum` is closed and content with a ripple running through it: a smile would say "pleased", but the point of `humming` is that the mouth is shut and something is coming out anyway. It is kept narrow so it does not read as a grin at sixteen pixels. `tight` is pressed shut — short, flat and a shade lower than `flat` — and `suspicious` wears it rather than `smirk`: suspicion is a mouth held shut while the eyes do the doubting, and a smirk reads as amusement next to a narrowed eye. `refusing` keeps its frown; that one is a refusal, not merely a reservation.

Six states talk: `composing`, `dictating`, `messaging`, `sending`, `laughing` and `humming`. Their mouth pulses open and shut on two incommensurate sine waves, so it reads as speech rather than as a metronome. The pulse is switched off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## One plate

The nose and mouth are projected with the same pose matrix as the eyes and take a share of the same gaze offset — 60% for the nose, 50% for the mouth. Turn the head and the whole face compresses as a unit; glance sideways and the features lean together. Without this the eyes would swing and the mouth would stay put, which reads as a mask sliding on a ball.

<LineShapeRow />

Both features are clamped inside the silhouette at their own height, and held above the chin on squat bodies, so a `tofu` or an `onigiri` gets a face that fits rather than one that hangs off the bottom edge. The [face and pose](/internals/face-and-pose#the-plate) internals cover the projection.

## Switching live

`face` can change on a mounted character. The eye size, the stroke floor and the feature opacity all ease on one `lineBlend` spring, so the mouth fades in as the eyes shrink rather than everything snapping. Changing `nose` crossfades the old nose out and the new one in. Switching to or from a drawn face runs the `faceFade` spring: the face being left fades out as it was at the moment of the switch while the new one fades in, and on a switch back to `cute` the cute eyes, which run underneath all along, take the face back.

```tsx preview
import { useState } from 'react';
import { CharacterMark, FACE_STYLES, NOSE_IDS } from '@aora-bot/react';

export default function Demo() {
  const [face, setFace] = useState(0);
  const [nose, setNose] = useState(0);
  return (
    <div className="site-demo site-demo--center">
      <CharacterMark
        shape="pear"
        color="green"
        state="happy"
        face={FACE_STYLES[face]}
        nose={NOSE_IDS[nose]}
        sizePx={130}
      />
      <div className="site-controls">
        <button className="site-btn" onClick={() => setFace((f) => (f + 1) % FACE_STYLES.length)}>
          face: {FACE_STYLES[face]}
        </button>
        <button className="site-btn" onClick={() => setNose((n) => (n + 1) % NOSE_IDS.length)}>
          nose: {NOSE_IDS[nose]}
        </button>
      </div>
    </div>
  );
}
```

## Where it applies

Every component that draws a character accepts `face` and `nose`: [`CharacterMark`](/components/character-mark), `StagedMark`, [`AgentPortrait`](/components/agent-portrait) (also through the agent object and through a cast preset), [`AvatarGroup`](/components/avatar-group) and the static renderers. A [`CharacterSelector`](/components/character-selector) with `onSelectFace` shows a face toggle, and a nose row when the line face is active.

On a [shared stage](/components/character-stage), face style and nose are part of the appearance key, so a `blob` with the `line` face and the `hook` nose and the same `blob` with the `bow` nose are two sources, while two `cute` blobs with different noses are one — only the line face draws a nose, so a nose on any other face is moot and does not split the stage.

## The line face across surfaces

Every component that puts a character on a surface — [`HudPlate`](/components/hud-plate) and [`AgentHud`](/components/agent-hud), the [editor](/components/avatar-studio) and its trigger, the [template dialog](/components/template-dialog) and share card, [`OccupancyList`](/components/occupancy-list), the [restricted scene](/components/restricted-scene) and [boot loop](/components/boot-loop), [`CastLineup`](/components/cast-lineup) and its members, the [walkthrough](/components/walkthrough) parts and both [pickers](/components/agent-character-selector) — takes `face`, `nose` and `lineWeight`, and passes them down unchanged. A character that is a line-face `bun` with a `hook` nose in the roster is the same line-face bun in the editor's preview, on the share card and in the boot sequence.

`lineWeight` is the third trait, and like the nose it is chosen once per character. It is a multiplier on `LINE_WEIGHT`, clamped to `LINE_WEIGHT_RANGE` (0.8–1.25), and one number reaches the nose, the mouth **and the HUD dials**: the ring outside the body is stroked at the same weight as the features inside it, so the face and its dial are one drawing rather than a face with a chart beside it. The range is deliberately narrow. The weight is the one number that decides whether a character reads as drawn with a pen or with a marker; letting a host set it freely would let one character in a list arrive at a different weight from its neighbours and break the set.

```tsx preview
import { CharacterMark, HudPlate } from '@aora-bot/react';

export default function Demo() {
  return (
    <div className="site-demo">
      {[0.8, 1, 1.25].map((weight) => (
        <HudPlate key={weight} title="lineWeight" value={String(weight)}>
          <CharacterMark
            shape="bun"
            color="orange"
            state="working"
            face="line"
            nose="hook"
            lineWeight={weight}
            hud={{ kind: 'progress', value: 0.65 }}
            sizePx={88}
          />
        </HudPlate>
      ))}
    </div>
  );
}
```

A [`CharacterSelector`](/components/character-selector) takes `lineWeight` too, so the previews in the grid are drawn with the same pen as the character being edited.
