# Faces

The face belongs to the character, not to a style. All three faces are drawn in the vector, pixel and kaomoji series alike, on the same states, poses and blinks. The default is `cute`.

```tsx
<CharacterMark shape="mochi" face="line" nose="hook" />
```

<FaceStyleRow />

| `face`           | Drawn as                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `cute` (default) | White dot eyes, a small mouth and pink blush painted on the body; some states also throw off a mood effect. See [Expressions](/design/expressions). |
| `eyes`           | Two cut-out eyes and nothing else — the silhouette does the rest.                                                                                   |
| `line`           | A nose and a mouth on top of the eyes, all strokes at one line weight.                                                                              |

`cute` is the default; pass `face="eyes"` or `face="line"` for the others. `eyes` and `line` have their own geometry and eye tables. Switching `face` on a mounted character does not jump: the `cute` face and the others cross over on the `cuteBlend` spring.

## cute: the default face

The `cute` face is painted on the body in `--aora-face` (white on every hue; see [Palette](/design/palette#variables-beyond-the-ink)). Every state has its own eyes, mouth and blush, and the eyes cycle on an interval. The tables and demos are on [Expressions](/design/expressions); the mood and activity effects of each state are on [States](/design/states).

On the `cute` face the belt states do not shrink the body to a bead: the whole character, face included, stays, and what the belt says on the other faces is said by an effect beside it.

## The line face

`line` adds a nose and a mouth to `eyes`. The sections below are about it: how it keeps one ink and one weight, what the mouth and nose are, and how they follow the state.

## One ink, one weight

The `eyes` face works because it is a single visual idea: a filled body and two cut-outs. Adding a nose and a mouth risks turning that into a drawing with several kinds of line on it. The line face avoids that by keeping the rule:

- Every feature is a stroke in the eye colour — the background on `solid`, the ink on `outline` and `glass`.
- Every stroke has the same weight, `LINE_WEIGHT` times the eye radius, so it scales with the body exactly as the eyes do.
- The eyes shrink towards dot proportions (`LINE_FACE_TUNE`) so three features share the plate instead of crowding it, and the thin expressions — `closed`, `happy`, `sleepy` — are floored to the line weight so they read as the same stroke as the mouth.
- There are no brows, no blush and no second colour.

<LineExpressionGrid />

Every eye topology of the `eyes` face is available on the line face; it only changes how they are sized and floored. `wink` is the one topology whose two eyes differ — a happy arc on one side, a neutral eye on the other.

## The mouth is six numbers

```ts
interface MouthSpec {
  width: number; // half-width, in eye radii
  bend: number; // bow of the lip line; positive smiles, negative frowns
  open: number; // height of the opening; 0 is a closed line
  wave: number; // one sine ripple along the lip, for unease
  skew: number; // pushes the apex towards one corner, −1..1
  dy: number; // vertical offset from the resting position
}
```

The lip line is a parabola through the two corners, bowed by `bend` with its apex pushed sideways by `skew`, plus one ripple of amplitude `wave`. The opening is a lens split unevenly — the lower lip drops further than the upper lip rises, the way a real mouth opens. When `open` is 0 the two lips coincide and only the stroke is visible, so a closed mouth opening into an "o" is one continuous shape rather than a swap.

<MouthGrid />

<MouthTable />

Like the eyes, the mouth is parametric for a reason: **morphing**. Every field interpolates independently and every intermediate value is a valid mouth, so a state change eases from `frown` to `grin` on a spring instead of cutting between two outlines.

```ts
morphMouth(MOUTHS.frown, MOUTHS.grin, t); // always a mouth
```

## The nose is chosen once

A nose is not an expression. It is a trait, like a shape, so it is chosen once per character and stays put while the eyes and mouth change.

<NoseGrid />

Six polylines. Each is a single open stroke down the bridge with a small turn for the tip, stored as sixteen points in eye-radius units. Passing `nose={null}` draws none — a plain line face with eyes and mouth only.

When no nose is given, one is derived from the character's key the same way its shape and ink are: `autoNoseForKey('agent-42')` is stable and spreads keys evenly across the six. `AgentAvatar` uses this, so every agent gets a consistent nose with nothing stored.

## Every state has a mouth

`STATE_MOUTH` names one mouth per state, in the same way `STATE_EYE_TOPOLOGIES` names the eyes. The eyes carry most of the expression already, so the mouth's job is to agree with them — a frown under `sad`, an "o" under `surprised`, a wavy line under `confused` — rather than say something on its own.

<LineStateGrid />

Two mouths exist for states the other eleven could not say honestly. `hum` is closed and content with a ripple running through it: a smile would say "pleased", but the point of `humming` is that the mouth is shut and something is coming out anyway. It is kept narrow so it does not read as a grin at sixteen pixels. `tight` is pressed shut — short, flat and a shade lower than `flat` — and `suspicious` wears it rather than `smirk`: suspicion is a mouth held shut while the eyes do the doubting, and a smirk reads as amusement next to a narrowed eye. `refusing` keeps its frown; that one is a refusal, not merely a reservation.

Six states talk: `composing`, `dictating`, `messaging`, `sending`, `laughing` and `humming`. Their mouth pulses open and shut on two incommensurate sine waves, so it reads as speech rather than as a metronome. The pulse is switched off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## One plate

The nose and mouth are projected with the same pose matrix as the eyes and take a share of the same gaze offset — 60% for the nose, 50% for the mouth. Turn the head and the whole face compresses as a unit; glance sideways and the features lean together. Without this the eyes would swing and the mouth would stay put, which reads as a mask sliding on a ball.

<LineShapeRow />

Both features are clamped inside the silhouette at their own height, and held above the chin on squat bodies, so a `tofu` or an `onigiri` gets a face that fits rather than one that hangs off the bottom edge. The [face and pose](/internals/face-and-pose#the-plate) internals cover the projection.

## Switching live

`face` can change on a mounted character. The eye size, the stroke floor and the feature opacity all ease on one `lineBlend` spring, so the mouth fades in as the eyes shrink rather than everything snapping. Changing `nose` crossfades the old nose out and the new one in.

```tsx preview
import { useState } from 'react';
import { CharacterMark, FACE_STYLES, NOSE_IDS } from '@aora-bot/react';

export default function Demo() {
  const [face, setFace] = useState(0);
  const [nose, setNose] = useState(0);
  return (
    <div className="site-demo site-demo--center">
      <CharacterMark
        shape="pear"
        color="green"
        state="happy"
        face={FACE_STYLES[face]}
        nose={NOSE_IDS[nose]}
        sizePx={130}
      />
      <div className="site-controls">
        <button className="site-btn" onClick={() => setFace((f) => (f + 1) % FACE_STYLES.length)}>
          face: {FACE_STYLES[face]}
        </button>
        <button className="site-btn" onClick={() => setNose((n) => (n + 1) % NOSE_IDS.length)}>
          nose: {NOSE_IDS[nose]}
        </button>
      </div>
    </div>
  );
}
```

## Where it applies

Every component that draws a character accepts `face` and `nose`: [`CharacterMark`](/components/character-mark), `StagedMark`, [`AgentAvatar`](/components/agent-avatar) (also through the agent object and through a cast preset), [`AvatarStack`](/components/avatar-stack) and the static renderers. A [`CharacterPicker`](/components/character-picker) with `onSelectFace` shows a face toggle, and a nose row when the line face is active.

On a [shared stage](/components/mark-stage), face style and nose are part of the appearance key, so a `blob` with the `line` face and the `hook` nose and the same `blob` with the `bow` nose are two sources, while two `cute` blobs with different noses are one — only the line face draws a nose, so a nose on any other face is moot and does not split the stage.

## The line face across surfaces

Every component that puts a character on a surface — [`HudPlate`](/components/hud-plate) and [`AgentHud`](/components/agent-hud), the [editor](/components/avatar-editor) and its trigger, the [template dialog](/components/template-dialog) and share card, [`OccupancyList`](/components/occupancy-list), the [restricted scene](/components/restricted-scene) and [boot loop](/components/boot-loop), [`CastStage`](/components/cast-stage) and its members, the [walkthrough](/components/walkthrough) parts and both [pickers](/components/agent-character-picker) — takes `face`, `nose` and `lineWeight`, and passes them down unchanged. A character that is a line-face `bun` with a `hook` nose in the roster is the same line-face bun in the editor's preview, on the share card and in the boot sequence.

`lineWeight` is the third trait, and like the nose it is chosen once per character. It is a multiplier on `LINE_WEIGHT`, clamped to `LINE_WEIGHT_RANGE` (0.8–1.25), and one number reaches the nose, the mouth **and the HUD dials**: the ring outside the body is stroked at the same weight as the features inside it, so the face and its dial are one drawing rather than a face with a chart beside it. The range is deliberately narrow. The weight is the one number that decides whether a character reads as drawn with a pen or with a marker; letting a host set it freely would let one character in a list arrive at a different weight from its neighbours and break the set.

```tsx preview
import { CharacterMark, HudPlate } from '@aora-bot/react';

export default function Demo() {
  return (
    <div className="site-demo">
      {[0.8, 1, 1.25].map((weight) => (
        <HudPlate key={weight} title="lineWeight" value={String(weight)}>
          <CharacterMark
            shape="bun"
            color="orange"
            state="working"
            face="line"
            nose="hook"
            lineWeight={weight}
            hud={{ kind: 'progress', value: 0.65 }}
            sizePx={88}
          />
        </HudPlate>
      ))}
    </div>
  );
}
```

A [`CharacterPicker`](/components/character-picker) takes `lineWeight` too, so the previews in the grid are drawn with the same pen as the character being edited.
